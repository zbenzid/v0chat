## 1. Hooks: taxonomies, what top practitioners say makes a hook work, and measurable targets

### Takeaway
The dominant 2024-2026 model is a multi-layer hook (visual + on-screen text + spoken line, all pointing at the same idea, with the visual and written layers ranked above the spoken one), delivered inside 1-3 seconds and immediately followed by a foreshadow of the payoff. Measurable targets exist but are inconsistent: the only large public Instagram benchmark puts average 3-second skip rate at about 60-66% for brand accounts, paid "hook rate" benchmarks cluster around 25-30% (30%+ strong on Reels), and YouTube Shorts practitioners point to 70-90% "viewed vs swiped away"; no platform publishes an official target.

### Cited Findings

**A. Layered ("triple") hook taxonomy**
- [PRACTITIONER] Kallaway (own X post): the non-obvious lever is "hook alignment". The spoken hook (what you say) is only the 3rd most important aspect; the visual hook (what you show) and the written/text hook rank above it. He advises starting ideation by finding the key visual first, then writing the spoken hook to match — [Kallaway on X](https://x.com/kanekallaway/status/1912316912198943206)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Kallaway's three components as summarised: visual hook = what you show (B-roll with motion, something that stops the scroll); spoken hook = a first line that opens a curiosity loop; text hook = larger overlay text that reinforces the spoken hook and is *not* the captions. All three must be aligned so the viewer is not confused — [Creator Spotlight profile of Kallaway](https://www.creatorspotlight.com/p/kallaway); [Recapio summary of "How to Create a Killer Hook"](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-a-killer-hook-impossible-to-skip-by-kallaway); (attribution between these two pages not confirmed)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) One summary reports Kallaway considers visual hooks significantly more powerful than spoken hooks — [briefread summary](https://briefread.pages.dev/en/summary/how-to-create-irresistible-hooks-and)
- [PRACTITIONER] French-language definition: on Instagram the hook is the first image + the on-screen text + the first spoken sentence, over one to three seconds. A good hook must, in that time, show who the video is for, give a reason to stay, and hint at a benefit — [CreatorsJet FR](https://www.creatorsjet.com/fr/blog/15-modeles-de-hooks-tiktok)
- [PRACTITIONER] One generic definition extends the package to five elements: image, motion, sound, text and meaning — [search result set on Hook-Retain-Reward, likely True Future Media](https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/science-of-short-form-video-hooks)
- [PRACTITIONER] Layer contrast (not from Kallaway): the on-screen text can tease the outcome while the spoken hook tells a story, or pose the method as a question while the spoken hook states the outcome — [socialk.it](https://socialk.it/en/blog/video-hooks-first-three-seconds)
- [PRACTITIONER] French source: the 3-second limit applies only to the spoken hook; what follows belongs to the body of the video — [BIGVU FR](https://bigvu.tv/blog/fr/accroches-pour-les-r-seaux-sociaux-960/)

**B. Hook-type taxonomies**
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Kallaway's spoken-hook formula in three beats: (1) "context lean" — define the topic clearly so the viewer knows what the video covers; (2) "scroll-stop interjection" — contrasting words that create a pause; (3) "contrarian snapback" — say something that pulls the viewer away from where they were leaning. Also circulated as the "Triple Hook Method". No source gives a per-step timing — [Podwise summary](https://podwise.ai/episodes/2639392); [Digital Zoom Studio](https://digitalzoomstudio.net/2026/11/the-triple-hook-method-set-context-make-them-lean-then-snap-them-back/)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary, low reliability: course resellers and fan-built AI "skills") Kallaway's six hook archetypes: Fortune Teller, Experimenter, Teacher, Magician, Investigator, Contrarian — [skills.lc Kallaway hook master](https://skills.lc/longyenkai83/personal-marketing-kit/longyenkai83-personal-marketing-kit-skills-2-content-kallaway-hook-master-skill-md)
- [PRACTITIONER] French five-family classification: curiosité, controverse, émotionnel, valeur/tutoriel, rupture visuelle — [go-viral.app FR](https://www.go-viral.app/fr/blog/meilleurs-hooks-pour-videos-courtes/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Other recurring French categories: question directe, erreur à éviter, liste, storytelling, contradiction — [Privize](https://privize.io/blog/hook-reel-instagram-viral); [Agence Midnight](https://agencemidnight.fr/hook/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Match hook type to content type: a tutorial needs clarity, a transformation needs contrast, a "mistake" video needs tension — [CreatorsJet FR](https://www.creatorsjet.com/fr/blog/15-modeles-de-hooks-tiktok)
- [DATA STUDY] (vendor) OpusClip, 34,635 clips, Jan-Mar 2026, proprietary hook taxonomy: "Project & Product Showcase" hooks led at an average 6,037 views per clip, roughly 2x the lowest-ranked hook type; the best type shows the result in the first 2 seconds (= result-first) — [OpusClip, 5 TikTok hook types](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-hooks-that-go-viral-2026)
- [DATA STUDY] (vendor) OpusClip: "shock/surprise" openers were the single most common hook in high-performing clips — [OpusClip, Anatomy of a viral TikTok 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/anatomy-of-a-viral-tiktok-2026)

### Inferences
- Candidate pass/fail checks for a script, derived from the above (not themselves sourced rules):
  1. The script specifies three hook layers separately (first frame / visual action, on-screen text hook, first spoken line) — fail if only a spoken line is written.
  2. The three layers express the same idea (Kallaway alignment); the text hook is not a transcript of the spoken line and not the captions.
  3. The spoken hook is finished within 3 seconds, the topic is identifiable in the first second, and a foreshadow of the payoff follows within the next 3 seconds (Hoyos).
  4. The hook is classifiable into one named type (result-first, contrarian, curiosity gap, question, negative, list, story cold-open, audience call-out, proof).
  5. The hook is understandable with sound off (Hoyos) — the visual plus text hook must carry the promise alone.
  6. Reading level about fifth grade: short common words, no jargon in the first line.
- Result-first/"showcase" hooks have the best vendor-data support (OpusClip); contrarian and curiosity-gap hooks have mostly practitioner support. A rubric could weight "payoff or proof visible in the first 2 seconds" higher than verbal cleverness.
- For post-publication calibration, a realistic Instagram skip-rate target is "beat about 60-65%" (Socialinsider average), not "below 20-30%"; the stricter figures circulating in blogs appear to have no data behind them.
- "Hook rate" (paid, 3-second plays ÷ impressions) and organic "3-second hold" / "skip rate" are different denominators; they should not be merged into one threshold.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for a hook **word count** rule (for example "≤ 10 words") or a per-layer timing. The only figure is a third-party "1-3 sentences maximum" attributed to Kallaway by a fan-built file — [skills.lc](https://skills.lc/AI-Answer/ContentRepurposeSystem/ai-answer-contentrepurposesystem-skills-hooks-skill-md).
- Nothing found on Yomi Denzel, Antoine BM, Thomas Burbidge, "Le Chairman" or Oussama Ammar-style creators' hook methods; search returned only reputation pieces. Nothing found on Roberto Blake or Brock Johnson / Personal Brand Launch, and nothing on "Hook Agency".
- Kallaway's and Hoyos's own videos/transcripts could not be opened; everything is from third-party summaries plus one Kallaway X post.
- The full French pages (CreatorsJet "100+ hooks", Metricool PDF, Submagic FR, Blog du Modérateur, Swello, Agorapulse) could not be opened, so the French list above is a small sample, and one example may be a translation.

## 2. Most common hook mistakes

### Takeaway
The mistakes that sources agree on are: spending the opening on anything other than the promise (greeting, logo, context), misaligned hook layers that confuse the viewer, complex language, and an opening that does not deliver what the packaging promised. Evidence is practitioner consensus, not measured data.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Recurrent advice across vendor guides: skip greetings and logo intros; open with a curiosity or contrast hook — [Grow-it scripting blueprint](https://www.grow-it.se/en/resources/short-form-video-scripting-retention-hooks); [Go-viral](https://www.go-viral.app/blog/best-hooks-for-short-form-video/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Misalignment between visual, text and spoken hook confuses viewers; fixing alignment is presented as a bigger lever than rewriting the spoken line — [Kallaway on X](https://x.com/kanekallaway/status/1912316912198943206); [Film and Flow newsletter, "you've been fixing the wrong part of your hook"](https://filmandflow.beehiiv.com/p/you-ve-been-fixing-the-wrong-part-of-your-hook)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary, low reliability) "Complex language kills hooks"; keep the spoken hook to 1-3 plain sentences — [skills.lc file built on Kallaway's framework](https://skills.lc/AI-Answer/ContentRepurposeSystem/ai-answer-contentrepurposesystem-skills-hooks-skill-md)
- [PRACTITIONER] Starting from a standstill rather than mid-action — [go-viral.app FR](https://www.go-viral.app/fr/blog/accroche-3-premieres-secondes/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Delay before the first useful point: time your delivery and remove it — [Sumera](https://sumera.io/blog/youtube-hook-formulas-script-examples)
- [PRACTITIONER] (long-form) MrBeast doc: the opening must confirm the promise of the title/thumbnail; the doc reports "We lost 21 million viewers in the first minute of the video" while noting that retention was still above platform average — [Sharp Daily](https://thesharpdaily.com/mrbeast-leaked-production-document-youtube-viral-success); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Kane: curiosity "without creating confusion" — a hook that is unclear fails even if intriguing — [NAR summary of Hook Point](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/you-have-just-3-seconds-to-hook-clients-with-your-real-estate-message)
- [DATA STUDY] (vendor, origin unclear) 50-60% of viewers who drop off do so within the first three seconds ("data from thousands of Shorts", no named source) — [OpusClip, The science of hooks](https://www.opus.pro/blog/the-science-of-hooks); flagged as OpusClip's own analysis by [QuickReel](https://quickreel.io/blog/clip-hook-length-data)
- [OFFICIAL] Google ABCD: "Get to the heart of the story faster" — [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Script-level fail conditions: first spoken words are a greeting, a name/brand introduction or "today I'm going to…"; any sentence of background precedes the promise; the hook promises something the body never delivers or delivers something different (hook/payoff mismatch); the first frame is a static talking head with no text; the hook needs a second read or sound to be understood.
- Hook/content mismatch ("clickbait") is treated by every structural framework here (Hormozi's "Reward", Hoyos's "end right after delivering the promise of the hook", MrBeast's "confirm the thumbnail") as a retention failure, so a rubric can test it mechanically: write the promise of the hook as one sentence, and check that the last line of the script satisfies exactly that sentence.

### Gaps
- No data study found that quantifies the cost of a greeting/logo intro or of hook-content mismatch in short-form. All "mistake" lists are opinion.
- No community (Reddit, forums) reports with numbers could be retrieved; search returned only marketing blogs.

## 3. Retention structure: open loops, re-hooks, but/therefore, stakes, payoff placement, pattern interrupts, pacing, loops

### Takeaway
The best-attested structure for a short is hook → foreshadow → body advanced by "but / therefore" beats → payoff in the last second → hard stop with no sign-off. Numeric editing rules (an interrupt every N seconds) are widely quoted but disagree by an order of magnitude and rest on vendor data; the more defensible advice is to place interrupts where your own retention curve drops.

### Cited Findings

**Structure and causality**
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Jenny Hoyos method: hook; then tell viewers what is coming, what the conflict is and what to expect by the end (foreshadow); body built with "but"/"therefore" (some summaries say "but"/"so") to create continuing conflict and resolution; end immediately after delivering the promise of the hook — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook); [Demand Curve newsletter #187](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-187); [The Digital Heirloom](https://thedigitalheirloom.beehiiv.com/p/7-tips-viral-content-wildly-successful-18yearold-youtuber)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Hoyos writes the hook and the last line first, then fills in the middle (rough script or bullets) — [Creator Science episode page](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/); [videohighlight summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/As7abwNhG7Y)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Hoyos: the last second of a (34-second) Short is crucial because that is where the payoff sits — [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/what-makes-jenny-hoyos-shorts-so-viral-by-moneyboymax)
- [PRACTITIONER] South Park rule (Trey Parker / Matt Stone): between any two beats the connective must be "therefore" or "but", never "and then"; if "and then" fits, the script is in trouble — [David Perell note](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/); [Cheat Sheet](https://www.cheatsheet.com/entertainment/south-park-trey-parker-and-matt-stone-1-rule.html/)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Hormozi's "Retain" has three methods — list, steps or story — and every sentence in the retain section must either advance the promise or build toward the reward — [Hook-Retain-Reward notes](https://margin-notes-site.pages.dev/p/framework/content-unit-hook-retain-reward.html)
- [PRACTITIONER] Short-form story skeleton: context → tension → process → payoff — [jamie.beehiiv, hook formula](https://jamie.beehiiv.com/p/the-hook-formula-you-need-to-skyrocket-retention) (attribution within the result set not confirmed)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Kane's "Jenga theory": build and release tension at the right moments — [SPOR write-up](https://www.wearespor.com/post/inside-the-viral-playbook-brendan-kane-s-strategic-approach-to-content-that-spreads)
- **Disagreement on payoff placement:** a book summary says Kane recommends giving away the punchline early in videos — [Summaries.com on Hook Point](https://summaries.com/blog/hook-point); Hoyos places the payoff at the very end and MrBeast's doc lists "payoffs at the end" formats (Last to Leave, stair-stepping) as retention devices — [GitHub gist summary of the MrBeast doc](https://gist.github.com/cognitivetech/55870132c63d8f456f8b550d0d6127a3). OpusClip's data favours showing the result in the first 2 seconds — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-hooks-that-go-viral-2026)
- [PRACTITIONER] (long-form) MrBeast doc: planned "re-engagements" — a spectacle or unexpected event that fits the story around minute 3, and another around minute 6 that drives the back half; "no dull moments" — [longform copy of the memo](https://longform.sayan.ee/employee-beast-production); [Alexander Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); [GitHub gist summary](https://gist.github.com/cognitivetech/55870132c63d8f456f8b550d0d6127a3)

- [PRACTITIONER] Shorts count each automatic loop as another view, so a seamless or twist ending can push average view percentage above 100% — [Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/looping-structure-shorts-retention-2026); [Joyspace](https://joyspace.ai/looping-hack-trick-algorithm-double-views)
- [OFFICIAL] (via guides) Since 31 March 2025 every play or replay of a Short counts as a view with no minimum watch time; the stricter older definition survives as "engaged views" — [Buffer guide to Shorts analytics](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics/); [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Traditional closers such as "thanks for watching" prompt viewers to swipe before the end — [Wreltik](https://wreltik.com/en/learn/reels/psychology-of-looping-video); [Influencers Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/loop-optimized-reels-the-seamless-cut-that-boosts-rewatch-ra/) (per-page attribution not confirmed)
- [PRACTITIONER] Loop craft: first and last frames should match in composition and energy, with audio that resolves as it restarts — [Influencers Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/loop-optimized-reels-the-seamless-cut-that-boosts-rewatch-ra/)
- [DATA STUDY] (UNVERIFIED) seamless loops on sub-7-second Reels: +18.7% replay rate, attributed to an Emplifi study not located — [SMMNut](https://smmnut.com/blog/tiktok-loop-content-strategy-2025/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Caveat: above-100% retention is a counting effect, and platforms publish no rewatch weighting — search-tool synthesis of [ViralWatch](https://viralwatch.app/viralwatch-video-analysis-blog/what-is-replay-value-in-short-form-video/)

### Inferences
- Script-level checks that follow:
  1. **And-then test:** between every pair of consecutive beats, "but" or "therefore/so" must fit; any "and then" join fails.
  2. **Foreshadow present:** one sentence within the first ~5 seconds that tells the viewer what they will get by the end.
  3. **Payoff last:** the sentence that resolves the hook is the final sentence; nothing follows it (no recap, no goodbye, no "follow for more" after it).
  4. **Every sentence earns its place:** each sentence advances the promise or builds toward the reward (Hormozi); delete-test each line.
  5. **One retain method:** the body is a list, a sequence of steps, or a story — not a mixture.
  6. **Visual change plan:** the script marks a visual change (cut, B-roll, zoom, text change) at a chosen cadence; given the conflict in the data (2-3 s vs 3-5 s vs 10-15 s), a defensible pass line is "no stretch longer than about 5 seconds without a marked visual change, and at least 2 shots in the first 5 seconds", stated as a house rule rather than as an established fact.
- The payoff-placement conflict resolves if "result-first hook" and "payoff at end" are treated as different objects: show the *outcome* up front (proof, result, the thing itself) and keep the *explanation or twist* (how, why, the catch) for the last second. This reading is mine; no source states it this way.
- The MrBeast "re-engagement at minute 3 and 6" idea scales down to a mid-video re-hook in a 30-60 s short, but the doc itself only speaks about long-form.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for explicit "open loop / re-hook every N seconds" rules in short-form, for "stakes escalation" as a named short-form rule, for "one idea per video", for sentence-length limits, or for dead-air/silence removal thresholds. These are common practitioner talking points but nothing citable surfaced.
- No independent (non-vendor) study of cut frequency or caption style versus retention was found.
- "3-second, 8-second, 30-second checkpoints" as a named framework: nothing found.

## 4. Script frameworks for short-form, and ideal length / word count per format

### Takeaway
Frameworks converge on three parts (hook, a retained middle, a reward) and differ mainly in the middle; the best-specified short-form variant is Hoyos's hook-foreshadow-buts-payoff. On length the data conflicts sharply (7-15 s, 21-34 s, 30-45 s and 60-90 s are each called "best" by different datasets), so length should follow the idea, with word count derived from a 120-170 WPM delivery rate.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Hook-Retain-Reward (Hormozi, *$100M Leads*): hook = capture attention; retain = hold it via list, steps or story; reward = satisfy the promise that captured it. Planning questions include "what is the retain method?" — [Hook-Retain-Reward notes](https://margin-notes-site.pages.dev/p/framework/content-unit-hook-retain-reward.html); [Skool short-form masterclass](https://skool.com/shortformmasterclass/the-secret-to-creating-irresistible-content-hook-retain-and-reward)
- [PRACTITIONER] A TikTok creator's adaptation: hook the scroll in the first 3 seconds, retain through the middle by paying off curiosity in beats, reward the watch with a satisfying payoff — [faceless.so](https://faceless.so/blog/hook-story-offer-framework-for-short-videos) (also describes Hook-Story-Offer for short videos)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Hoyos: idea → write hook and last line → foreshadow → body with "but/therefore" → payoff/twist → stop — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook); [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Kallaway: three-beat hook (context lean → scroll-stop interjection → contrarian snapback) — [Podwise](https://podwise.ai/episodes/2639392)
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Kane: choose a proven format first, then fill it with your story — [SPOR write-up](https://www.wearespor.com/post/inside-the-viral-playbook-brendan-kane-s-strategic-approach-to-content-that-spreads)

**Length (conflicting data)**
- [PRACTITIONER] (secondary) Hoyos's Shorts average about 34 seconds — [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/what-makes-jenny-hoyos-shorts-so-viral-by-moneyboymax). The search tool found no primary confirmation of a fixed 34-second target.
- [DATA STUDY] Paddy Galloway (April 2023): most creators made Shorts of 20-40 seconds; the algorithm appeared to favour longer Shorts (40 s+) that hold viewers; Shorts with average view duration above 50 seconds averaged 4.1 million views; outliers existed at all lengths ("don't feel you have to make your video a certain length") — [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html)

- [PRACTITIONER/vendor] Other claimed sweet spots: TikTok 21-34 s; "viral range" 24-38 s; Reels 7-15 s with a sharp drop-off beyond 60 s; versus Reels of 60-90 s having the highest engagement and views (attributed to Sprout Social 2025 data) — [Joyspace](https://joyspace.ai/ideal-video-length-social-platform-2026); [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/video-length-sweet-spots-tiktok-reels-shorts); [Revid](https://www.revid.ai/blog/social-media-video-length-best-practices); [Benno Films](https://bennofilms.com/blog/ideal-instagram-tiktok-video-length) (per-page attribution not confirmed). **These directly contradict each other.**

- [PRACTITIONER] (UNVERIFIED) 42% of Shorts viewers watch to completion when the video is under 20 seconds — [loop/rewatch result set, e.g. Virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/looping-structure-shorts-retention-2026)

**Word count**
- [PRACTITIONER] 2 words per second planning rule (30 s ≈ 60 words; 60 s ≈ 120 words); energetic short-form delivery 150-170 WPM (30 s ≈ 75-85 words; 60 s ≈ 150-170 words) — [dev.to](https://dev.to/hammadcgi/how-to-estimate-video-length-from-script-word-count-28h5); [Teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/script-speed-tips-for-teleprompter-delivery)

### Inferences
- Word budgets by format, computed from the 120-170 WPM range above (arithmetic, not a sourced table): 7-15 s ≈ 14-40 words; 20-40 s ≈ 40-110 words; 60-90 s ≈ 120-255 words. For French, which tends to run longer than English for the same content, the lower half of each range is the safer budget (my assumption, not sourced).
- Because length data conflicts, a rubric should not pass/fail on duration itself but on density: "could any sentence be removed without breaking the hook's promise?" plus "does the word count fit the planned duration at ≤ 170 WPM with at least 1-2 seconds of slack?"
- The Buffer and Galloway results (longer does better on reach/views) and the completion-rate advice (shorter completes better) are compatible with Mosseri's statement that Instagram weighs both percentage watched and absolute seconds: a longer video only wins if it holds.

### Gaps
- No sourced short-form-specific treatment of PAS, AIDA or Hook-Value-CTA was retrieved (they are widely used, but nothing citable with operational detail surfaced before the search budget ran out).
- The Socialinsider, Metricool and Sprout Social length studies could not be opened; only Buffer's TikTok study is confirmed at snippet level.
- No source found giving word counts per format directly; the table above is derived.

## 5. On-screen text and captions

### Takeaway
Captions and an on-screen text hook are near-universal in high-performing short-form (about 80% of 13.5M analysed clips carry captions), and practitioners treat sound-off comprehension as mandatory, but the famous "85% watch muted" figure is old and weakly sourced and no platform has released true sound-on data for Reels, TikTok or Shorts.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA STUDY] (vendor) OpusClip: captions are used in 80.2% of all 13.5M clips analysed; 78.6% of clips use animated (word-by-word or pop-on) captions. This is prevalence, not a measured lift — [OpusClip caption strategy research](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-caption-strategy-short-form); [OpusClip editing tips](https://www.opus.pro/blog/video-editing-tips)
- [DATA STUDY] (small sample, practitioner scan) 75 of 79 Reels examined had on-screen text in the first 3 seconds — [Creator Lane, frame-by-frame anatomy](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/first-three-seconds-instagram-hook-anatomy-2026) (attribution within the result set not confirmed)
- [DATA STUDY] (UNVERIFIED) hook text present on the first frame lifts 3-second retention by about 50% ("OverlayText, 2026") — [Reels hook result set, e.g. Fobet Media](https://fobetmedia.com/instagram-reel-hooks/)
- [DATA STUDY] Verizon Media / Publicis Media, 2019: 69% of consumers watch video with sound off in public places, 25% keep it muted in private — still the origin of most "sound-off" claims; dated — [Manchester Digital / Title Productions](https://www.manchesterdigital.com/post/title-productions/mute-is-the-new-norm-why-captions-win-in-2025-video); [Newton Technologies](https://www.newtontech.net/en/blog/23083-captions-increase-viewership-accessibility-and-reach/)

- [DATA STUDY] (UNVERIFIED) "2025 study: 68% of TikTok views and 54% of Reels views begin with sound off or very low volume" — original study not found — [dev.to](https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9)

- [PRACTITIONER] Auto-captions are a fine start, but a written hook line usually reads clearer — [Coinis](https://coinis.com/how-to/tiktok-ad-creative-best-practices)
- [PRACTITIONER] Text hook ≠ captions: it is a larger overlay reinforcing the spoken hook — [Creator Spotlight on Kallaway](https://www.creatorspotlight.com/p/kallaway)

### Inferences
- Script-level checks: (1) a text hook is written as its own line in the script, distinct from the spoken hook; (2) the script is understandable with sound off — read only the on-screen text lines and check the promise and payoff survive; (3) captions are specified (animated/word-by-word is the prevailing style in the OpusClip sample); (4) all text placements are marked inside the safe zone (top 14% / bottom 35% / sides 6% clear).
- Text-hook length: no sourced limit was found. If the viewer decides in roughly 1.5 seconds (OpusClip's claim) and reads at an ordinary on-screen pace of 3-4 words per second, the text hook has to be about 5-7 words. The reading-speed figure is my assumption, so this number should be presented as a house rule, not a finding.
- The safest framing for a report: "design for sound-off comprehension" is supported by convention and by prevalence data, but "X% watch muted" should not be stated as a current fact for Reels or TikTok.

- No source found for "text hook readable in under 2 seconds" or a headline word limit.
- Organic (non-ad) Reels/TikTok/Shorts safe-zone specifications from the platforms themselves were not retrieved; the figures above are for Meta ads via third parties.

## 6. CTA design

### Takeaway
On Instagram the action worth designing for is the DM send (Mosseri names sends per reach as a top signal, and the stronger one for reaching non-followers), with saves as a secondary utility signal; explicit generic "like/comment/share" requests fall under Meta's engagement-bait demotion policy. No controlled data was found comparing CTA wordings or measuring the retention cost of an end CTA.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] (via trade press and summaries) Adam Mosseri, January 2025: the three signals that matter most are watch time, likes and sends; creators should watch average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach; "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content" — [Keywords Everywhere tracker](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/instagram-algorithm-updates/); [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/news/instagram-mosseri-ranking-signals-guidance); [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach)
- [PRACTITIONER] (UNVERIFIED) "sends carry 3-5x the weight of likes" appears only in secondary blogs — [Socialday](https://socialday.live/features/instagrams-dm-share-signal-now-drives-3-to-5-times-more-reach-than-likes)
- [PRACTITIONER] Saves signal personal utility and are reported as "useful but secondary"; reference-style content (lists, step-by-step guides) is what drives saves — [Virallized](https://www.virallized.com/how-to-get-more-saves-and-shares-on-instagram); [Influencers Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/instagram-saves-and-dm-shares-now-drive-reels-reach/)

- [PRACTITIONER] End-of-video sign-offs ("thanks for watching") trigger early swipes — [Wreltik](https://wreltik.com/en/learn/reels/psychology-of-looping-video); Hoyos: end right after the payoff — [Demand Curve](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-187)
- [DATA STUDY] Paddy Galloway (Shorts, 2023): engagement metrics "don't seem massively important" for Shorts distribution (flagged as preliminary) — [Paddy Galloway on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898373365051392)
- [OFFICIAL] Google ABCD "Direction" is one of four pillars (clear call to action), built on ad-effectiveness measurement — [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Script-level checks: (1) at most one CTA; (2) the CTA names a specific person or occasion ("send this to the friend who…", "save this for your next shoot") rather than a generic "like, comment, share"; (3) a keyword-comment CTA must offer a concrete deliverable — a comment that triggers the delivery of a named resource is closer to a "specific call to action" than to bait, but Meta's published policy does not address this case, so treat it as a tolerated practice, not a guaranteed-safe one; (4) no spoken CTA after the payoff line — put it in the caption, on-screen text, or fold it into the payoff sentence, so the video can end and loop.
- A send-CTA fits content whose hook calls out a recognisable person or situation; a save-CTA fits list/step content. So the CTA type can be checked against the "retain method" chosen in section 3.
- On Shorts, CTA-driven engagement appears to matter less than on Instagram (Galloway), so spending seconds on a CTA is harder to justify there.

### Gaps
- No A/B or dataset evidence found on which CTA wording produces more saves, sends or comments, nor on CTA placement versus retention drop.
- Meta's policy text on whether "comment KEYWORD" funnels count as engagement bait was not found; neither was any 2025-26 Mosseri statement on it.
- TikTok and YouTube engagement-bait rules were not retrieved.

## 7. Data studies on hooks and retention

### Takeaway
There are a handful of usable datasets (Socialinsider's skip-rate benchmark, Buffer's TikTok length study, Galloway's Shorts study, TikTok/Kantar ad research, OpusClip's clip analyses), but most "statistics" circulating about the first three seconds cannot be traced to a primary source, and almost all quantified creative guidance comes from ads, not organic content.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA STUDY] Socialinsider (about 140,000 business Reels, Jan-Jun 2026): skip rate in the first 3 seconds averages 60.5%-65.5% by account size, larger accounts lower; skip rate = plays skipped in the first 3 seconds ÷ total plays × 100 — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/)
- [DATA STUDY] TikTok Marketing Science with Kantar (3,500 ads run in 2021, published 2022): the first six seconds capture 90% of cumulative ad recall; TikTok's own pages put the awareness figure at about 80% — [TikTok for Business blog on resonance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/resonance-key-factor-ad-effectiveness); [TikTok Creative Codes PDF, May 2023](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_CreativeCodes_May2023.pdf); summary via [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/short-form-video-statistics/). A claim that the first 2 seconds account for about half the impact could not be found on TikTok's pages.
- [DATA STUDY] (secondary, attributed to TikTok) 63% of the highest-CTR ads highlight their key message or product within the first three seconds; applies to ad creative, not organic For You videos — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/tiktok-ad-best-practices-improve-performance); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-creative-best-practices-2026). Not confirmed on TikTok's own page.
- [DATA STUDY] (UNVERIFIED, attributed to TikTok) "74% attention" and "3.3x action" figures; "71% decide in the first seconds"; "65% of viewers who watch the first 3 seconds keep watching at least 10 seconds"; a "1.4x completion-rate gain" — none traceable to a primary page — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/tiktok-ad-best-practices-improve-performance); [Teleprompter.com on the 3-second rule](https://teleprompter.com/blog/tiktok-3-second-rule)
- [DATA STUDY] (vendor) OpusClip: 34,635 clips (Jan-Mar 2026) by hook type, showcase/result-first hooks about 2x the weakest type; captions in 80.2% of 13.5M clips, animated in 78.6%; 50-60% of drop-off in the first 3 seconds; pain-point-specific openings beat generic ones by about 23% in retention (this last one second-hand and unconfirmed) — [OpusClip hook types](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-hooks-that-go-viral-2026); [OpusClip caption research](https://www.opus.pro/research/best-caption-strategy-short-form); [ShortsFast citing OpusClip](https://shortsfast.com/blog/faceless-shorts-hooks-retention-2026/)
- [DATA STUDY] Buffer (1.1M TikToks, March 2025): over-60-second videos +43.2% reach and +63.8% watch time versus 30-60 s — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/longer-tiktoks-get-more-views-data/)
- [DATA STUDY] Paddy Galloway (3.3B views, 5,400 Shorts, 33 channels, April 2023): viewed-vs-swiped 70-90% for top performers and under 60% rarely works; longer Shorts that hold do better; about 16.9 subscribers per 10,000 Shorts views — [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- [DATA STUDY] (vendor) Motion: average hook rate 25-30% for short-form video ads, top variants 40-50% — [Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/best-dtc-meta-ad-hooks-2025)
- [DATA STUDY] Google ABCD: built on 17,000+ campaigns measured with Ipsos, Nielsen and Kantar (third-party restatement); a Google case example: ads meeting over 80% of ABCD standards drove a 3-5% brand-awareness lift — [Think with Google YouTube playbook](https://business.google.com/en-all/think/search-and-video/youtube-marketing-success-playbook/); [MB Adv](https://www.mbadv.agency/youtube-ads/creating-effective-youtube-video-ads)
- [OFFICIAL] Instagram introduced skip rate and a per-Reel retention chart (August 2025), and the API metric (December 2025) — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/); [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/12/03/instragram-api-updates/). Whether skip rate is itself a ranking input is unconfirmed; one guide notes Instagram publishes no such relationship — [Pullsy](https://getpullsy.com/blog/reels-skip-rate)
- [OFFICIAL] (via guides) YouTube: from 31 March 2025 Shorts views count every play; "engaged views" keeps the old definition; "viewed vs swiped away" is reported in Studio with no official target — [Buffer guide](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics/); [PrePublish](https://prepublish.ai/blog/viewed-vs-swiped-away-youtube-shorts)

### Inferences
- A rubric can only cite three numbers with reasonable confidence: (a) Instagram's own definition of the hook window = first 3 seconds (skip rate); (b) TikTok's guidance = proposition in the first 3 seconds, hook inside the first 6; (c) Socialinsider's 60-66% average skip rate as the post-publication yardstick. Everything else (72% → 3.4x, 65% → 10 s, 1.7 s decision time, 50% lift from first-frame text) should be labelled unverified or left out.
- Most quantified "best practices" (TikTok/Kantar, Google ABCD, Motion, Meta safe zones) are ad research. Applying them to organic Reels is a reasonable transfer but is an assumption.
- Vendor datasets (OpusClip, Grow-it, inBeat) describe what their own users' clips look like; high prevalence of a feature (e.g. captions in 80% of clips) does not show the feature causes performance.

### Gaps
- TikTok's "What's Next" report and Creative Center data: not retrieved (the planned query was not run; ads.tiktok.com could not be opened).
- Meta's own Reels creative research (audio, human presence, 9:16 lift figures): nothing official surfaced in search.
- Metricool's and Sprout Social's annual studies: not retrieved.
- Community evidence (Reddit r/InstagramMarketing, r/Tiktokhelp, r/NewTubers, IndieHackers, Hacker News): **no usable thread was retrieved**; reddit.com and news.ycombinator.com were unreachable and search returned only marketing blogs. No [ANECDOTE]-tagged finding could be included in these notes.
